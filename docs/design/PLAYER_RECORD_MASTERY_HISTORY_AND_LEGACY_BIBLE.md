# Player Record, Mastery, History, and Legacy Bible

## Status

This document defines how *Beyond the Ridge* rewards skill, records history, supports completion, and satisfies a player over time.

It does not describe an implemented engine build.

## Purpose

The game needs more than meaningful choices.

A player also needs clear goals, responsive controls, visible improvement, real risk, satisfying completion, and reasons to replay.

The project therefore uses a layered Player Record instead of one universal score.

The system answers three questions.

1. How well did I perform the action?
2. What changed because of what I did?
3. What kind of history did this playthrough create?

## Player promise

> My hands improve. My choices change the world. The game remembers.

The game satisfies the player through five connected forms of progress.

- **Mastery:** The player becomes better at useful verbs.
- **Consequence:** The world changes in visible ways.
- **Discovery:** New places, people, evidence, and routes become available.
- **Ownership:** The player creates a distinct history rather than selecting a preset personality.
- **Replay:** The game supports personal bests, alternate approaches, different endings, and Second Roads.

## Core rule

The game has scores.

It does not have one morality score.

The game can score a repair, rescue, route, performance, or investigation because those activities have observable craft.

The game does not score faith, obedience, trauma, family loyalty, medical vulnerability, forgiveness, or whether Mara leaves an unsafe relationship.

## System name

The complete player-facing system is the **Living Record**.

The Living Record contains:

- encounter scores,
- practical outcomes,
- capability mastery,
- tool familiarity,
- material resources,
- map and place knowledge,
- relationship milestones,
- competing accounts of events,
- injuries and losses,
- Redemption Windows,
- endings,
- and campaign legacy.

The player opens the Living Record through the **Roadbook**.

## Satisfaction loop

```text
See a need
-> prepare an approach
-> perform under pressure
-> receive physical feedback
-> complete, fail, or withdraw
-> see the consequence
-> record the history
-> unlock a verb, route, relationship, or challenge
-> choose the next risk
```

The loop operates at several time scales.

| Time scale | Player satisfaction |
|---|---|
| Second to second | Responsive movement, grip, tool contact, steering, balance, sound, and readable reaction |
| Five to fifteen minutes | A completed task, a clear loss, an encounter score, or a meaningful discovery |
| Thirty to sixty minutes | A new capability, route, ally, tool, institution, or relationship move |
| Act | A changed map, competing history, major loss, path commitment, and act summary |
| Campaign | A distinct ending, Journey Score, Legacy Card, Ending Archive, and possible Second Road |

## Layer 1: live action feedback

The game responds before it displays a score.

The player should feel success and failure through:

- tool fit,
- material movement,
- engine rhythm,
- horse response,
- traction,
- balance,
- character posture,
- changed access,
- and environmental state.

The interface confirms what the world already communicates.

A score cannot rescue an unreadable interaction.

## Layer 2: encounter score

Mechanical, traversal, rescue, investigation, performance, and selected care encounters use a twelve-mark score.

The score appears after the immediate danger or conversation settles.

It does not interrupt the decisive action.

### Mark scale

Each measure receives zero to three marks.

| Marks | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | The player missed, abandoned, or contradicted the measure |
| 1 | The player achieved part of the measure with major weakness |
| 2 | The player completed the measure competently |
| 3 | The player completed the measure with strong control and understanding |

### Encounter ranks

| Total | Rank |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Unfinished |
| 4 to 6 | Rough |
| 7 to 9 | Sound |
| 10 to 11 | Skilled |
| 12 | Masterwork |

The rank describes performance in the encounter.

It does not judge Mara as a person.

### Work and repair score

- Precision
- Safety
- Resourcefulness
- Verification

### Traversal and driving score

- Route reading
- Control
- Conservation
- Recovery

### Rescue score

- Awareness
- Stabilization
- Protection
- Handoff

### Investigation score

- Source quality
- Comparison
- Restraint
- Record

### Performance and study score

- Preparation
- Timing
- Adaptation
- Completion

### Care score

A care encounter can score process without scoring a belief or treatment preference.

- Observation
- Stabilization
- Monitoring
- Handoff

The factual situation determines whether home care, outside advice, clinic referral, or emergency transport is appropriate.

The score does not award points merely for agreeing with one character.

## Score presentation

A completed work encounter can display:

```text
THE HAIRLINE CRACK

PRECISION          3
SAFETY             2
RESOURCEFULNESS    3
VERIFICATION       1

WORK MARK          9 / 12
RANK               SOUND

OUTCOME
Temporary repair
Road test incomplete
Evidence preserved
```

The scorecard separates performance from outcome.

A skilled action can still occur inside a costly story result.

A poor action can occasionally benefit from luck.

The game records both.

## Canon score and replay score

The story run records the first resolved encounter result as the **Canon Mark**.

The player can later replay the encounter from the Challenge Board.

The Challenge Board records the **Best Mark** separately.

Replay does not rewrite the story state.

This rule protects consequence while still supporting mastery.

## No score for manufactured suffering

The game does not award points for:

- causing an injury,
- triggering a death,
- remaining in danger,
- creating a wrongful accusation,
- losing a scholarship,
- activating a Redemption Window,
- or enduring abuse.

A player can discover those branches for narrative completion.

The Journey Score does not reward the loss itself.

## Accessibility and score

Accessibility settings do not reduce scores.

The player can use:

- hold or toggle inputs,
- simplified balance,
- extended response windows,
- game-speed adjustment,
- high-contrast hazards,
- sound-direction indicators,
- larger text,
- and controller remapping.

Difficulty and accessibility remain separate.

## Layer 3: capability mastery

Generic experience points do not define Mara's growth.

The game tracks demonstrated capability.

### Capability tracks

- Repair
- Fieldcraft
- Care
- Research
- Performance
- Voice

### Mastery tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unfamiliar | Mara needs direct prompts or another person's guidance |
| Practiced | Mara can perform basic verbs in a familiar setting |
| Capable | Mara can plan and complete a normal task |
| Reliable | Mara can adapt under pressure and verify the result |
| Expert | Mara can improvise, coordinate, and transfer the skill |
| Mentor | Mara can teach, lead, and create safer systems for others |

### Mastery proofs

A capability advances through four kinds of proof.

1. **Practice:** Use the verb successfully in more than one encounter.
2. **Pressure:** Use the verb while conditions change.
3. **Transfer:** Apply the skill in a new place or domain.
4. **Verification:** Confirm that the result works.

The Roadbook shows the next proof that the player can pursue.

It does not require empty repetition.

### Example repair progression

| Tier | New capability |
|---|---|
| Practiced | Identify common wear and select basic tools |
| Capable | Plan a sequence and remove or replace a component |
| Reliable | Adapt under pressure and verify under controlled load |
| Expert | Fabricate, diagnose interacting faults, and direct shared work |
| Mentor | Teach, inspect, design jigs, and establish safer work methods |

### Capability rewards

Mastery can unlock:

- new verbs,
- faster setup,
- clearer material information,
- safer alternatives,
- reduced sway or error,
- new dialogue authority,
- cooperative actions,
- new contracts,
- and transformed Second Roads.

Mastery does not increase a generic damage number.

## Tool, vehicle, and horse familiarity

Individual tools and companions retain familiarity.

### Familiarity states

- Handled
- Familiar
- Trusted

Familiarity can improve:

- selection speed,
- animation confidence,
- control stability,
- inspection detail,
- recovery from mistakes,
- and access to alternate techniques.

A trusted tool can still break.

A trusted horse can still refuse dangerous ground.

The system rewards knowledge without making the world harmless.

## Layer 4: material progression

The player manages a small, meaningful economy.

Tracked resources can include:

- cash,
- store credit,
- fuel,
- parts,
- food,
- medicine,
- time,
- favors,
- shelter,
- and information.

The player earns resources through work, performances, repairs, teaching, research, and community tasks.

The player spends resources on tools, transport, study, care, access, repairs, and recovery.

The economy creates choices.

It does not become an inventory-cleaning simulator with a family drama attached.

## Layer 5: map and discovery progress

The map tracks how Mara knows a place.

### Place knowledge states

- Heard of
- Located
- Visited
- Understood
- Connected

### Route knowledge states

- Rumored
- Marked
- Traveled
- Reliable
- Mastered

Each map entry records its source.

Examples include:

- Mara's observation,
- Jonah's sketch,
- Nora's directions,
- a county record,
- a bus route,
- or a college schedule.

The player can pursue regional completion without receiving an omniscient map.

## Layer 6: relationship history

Relationships do not use one friendship bar.

The Living Record tracks milestones in:

- trust,
- respect,
- fear,
- loyalty,
- dependence,
- and clarity.

The player sees meaningful events instead of raw numbers.

Examples include:

- Abel relied on Mara's diagnosis.
- Eliza repeated Mara's account in private.
- Ruth stopped using a childhood nickname after the clinic call.
- Caleb offered transport without asking for an explanation.
- Tess trusted Mara with the night garage key.

Relationship history records:

- promises,
- shared work,
- private names,
- refusals,
- absences,
- help,
- testimony,
- boundaries,
- and unresolved conflict.

## Layer 7: the Living History

The game records history as competing accounts.

It does not pretend that every event has one visible, complete truth.

### History views

The Roadbook can display:

- **Mara saw:** Direct observation and physical traces.
- **Mara heard:** Testimony, rumor, teaching, or accusation.
- **Mara concluded:** Her current interpretation.
- **Mara revised:** A later interpretation that preserves the earlier version.
- **Household account:** What the family says happened.
- **Public account:** What the town, institution, or official record says happened.
- **Known consequence:** What visibly changed.

### Example history entry

```text
AUGUST 18 — LOWER SALVAGE YARD

MARA SAW
The chalk line moved before Eli touched the chain.

MARA HEARD
Abel said Eli caused the shift.
Tess heard the engine change before the warning.

HOUSEHOLD ACCOUNT
Eli moved too quickly under a stable load.

PUBLIC ACCOUNT
No report filed.

MARA CONCLUDED
The chain had already begun to slip.

KNOWN CONSEQUENCE
Eli's shoulder injured.
Quarry contract delayed.
Eliza trust increased.
Abel fear increased.
```

### Causal links

The game can connect events when Mara has enough support.

Examples include:

- temporary axle repair -> later vibration,
- unsecured cargo -> rain-road loss,
- missed weather sign -> ridge exposure,
- overstated conclusion -> wrongful accusation.

The game does not reveal hidden future consequences.

It adds the link after the relationship becomes observable.

### History can remain wrong

The official record can remain false.

The family version can harden.

Mara can revise her own conclusion without changing what other people believe.

This tension is part of the game state, not a failure of the journal.

### Map

- closures,

### People

Shows:

- forms of address,
- shared work actions,
- promises,

### Record

Shows:

- event chronology,
- competing accounts,
- evidence states,

## Layer 8: Journey Score

The Journey Score is an optional campaign score.

It appears at the end of an act and at the end of a run.

The player can hide it during a first story playthrough.

### Base score

The base score has a maximum of 1,000 points.

| Category | Maximum | What it measures |
|---|---:|---|
| Mastery | 250 | Encounter marks and demonstrated capability |
| Discovery | 200 | Places, routes, evidence, and hidden observations |
| Record | 200 | Evidence preservation, source comparison, uncertainty, and revision |
| Stewardship | 150 | Fuel, parts, equipment, time, and resource management |
| Challenges | 200 | Optional contracts, conditions, feats, and mastery replays |

### Difficulty modifier

| Mode | Modifier |
|---|---:|
| Story | 1.00 |
| Standard | 1.15 |
| Frontier | 1.35 |
| Iron Ridge | 1.60 |

Custom mode displays its selected modifiers.

Accessibility settings do not reduce the modifier.

### What the Journey Score does not include

The Journey Score does not award or subtract points for:

- religious belief,
- family loyalty,
- romantic choice,
- forgiveness,
- leaving home,
- staying near the mountain,
- attending college,
- choosing medical care,
- experiencing trauma,
- or accepting a Second Road.

The score cannot determine a good ending.

## Personal bests and comparison

The first implementation supports personal bests.

The system can compare:

- Canon Mark,
- Best Mark,
- first-attempt result,
- no-injury result,
- low-resource result,
- reduced-HUD result,
- and difficulty result.

The first prototype does not require an online leaderboard.

A future social comparison must remain opt-in.

It must not rank private story choices.

## Feats and achievements

Feats recognize unusual mastery, discovery, and persistence.

They do not function as hidden morality points.

Examples include:

- **Measure Twice:** Complete five verified repairs.
- **Weather Eye:** Turn back before exposure becomes critical and return successfully later.
- **One More Version:** Revise a conclusion after new evidence.
- **No Loose Ends:** Verify every major work task in one act.
- **The Second Record:** Preserve a disputed record through a major loss.
- **Different Hands:** Complete a transformed-capability Second Road.
- **The Road Did Not End:** Complete a Second Road without restoring the original goal.
- **What She Left:** Complete a Legacy Chapter.

The game does not create achievements for enduring abuse, causing preventable injury, or withholding medical help.

## Challenge Board

The Challenge Board separates replay mastery from canon history.

After the player resolves an encounter, the board can offer variants.

Possible modifiers include:

- different weather,
- fewer tools,
- damaged equipment,
- another companion,
- reduced HUD,
- tighter time,
- heavier load,
- no map,
- or a harder verification condition.

The player can chase a Masterwork score without rewriting the story.

## Run paths and endings

The game supports several viable campaign paths.

Examples include:

- workshop and trade,
- research and academic work,
- performance and teaching,
- care and public service,
- community leadership,
- and a local independent path with clear boundaries.

The Ending Archive records the path and its cost.

It does not rank one vocation above another.

## Legacy Card

Each completed run creates a Legacy Card.

The card summarizes the player's distinct history.

### Legacy Card fields

- Ending title
- Chosen path
- Difficulty
- Journey Score
- Highest capabilities
- Masterwork encounters
- Regions understood
- Major allies
- Major losses
- Official account
- Mara's final account
- Unresolved questions
- Redemption Windows discovered
- Second Roads completed
- Legacy Chapter status
- Unique run identifier

### Legacy Card headings

The final presentation can use five human-readable headings.

- What you built
- What you lost
- What the world says
- What Mara knows
- What remains possible

The card does not label the run good, bad, correct, or canonical.

## Gamer satisfaction map

| Player need | System response |
|---|---|
| Competence | Tactile actions, encounter marks, capability tiers, tool familiarity |
| Autonomy | Multiple approaches, safe withdrawal, transformed goals, different campaign paths |
| Risk | Injury, death, scarcity, missed opportunities, permadeath, and persistent loss |
| Curiosity | Unmapped routes, competing accounts, hidden seeds, institutions, and unresolved questions |
| Relatedness | Multi-axis relationships, shared work, private names, help, boundaries, and memory |
| Completion | Region states, feats, Masterwork encounters, endings, and the Archive |
| Self-expression | Starting tendencies, loadouts, priorities, dialogue intent, and chosen vocation |
| Replay | Challenge Board, personal bests, alternate leads, difficulty modes, endings, and Second Roads |
| Narrative ownership | Living History, branch saves, Legacy Card, and persistent consequences |

## Why this is satisfying as a game

The player does not only watch Mara change.

The player learns difficult controls and becomes better at using them.

The player can prepare well, react quickly, improvise, retreat, or fail.

The player sees tools, routes, people, and institutions respond to prior action.

The player earns useful verbs instead of generic power.

The player can compare performance without reducing human choices to points.

The player can finish with a history that another player did not create.

The player can lose and still discover a credible reason to continue.

## Prototype proof

The first prototype must include:

1. One encounter scorecard.
2. One Canon Mark and Best Mark comparison.
3. One capability advancement with visible mastery proofs.
4. One tool-familiarity change.
5. One map entry with a recorded source.
6. One relationship milestone.
7. One event with competing household and public accounts.
8. One known causal link.
9. One persistent loss in the Living History.
10. One Challenge Board replay.
11. One act-level Journey Score.
12. One Legacy Card mockup.

## Player test gates

The system passes a youth review when:

- At least 80 percent can explain the immediate objective.
- At least 75 percent understand why an encounter received its marks.
- At least 70 percent can identify a capability they want to improve.
- At least 70 percent can describe one action-to-consequence link.
- At least 60 percent voluntarily choose an encounter replay.
- At least 70 percent want to continue after the session.
- Fewer than 15 percent interpret the Journey Score as a morality score.
- No reviewer believes an accessibility option lowered the score.
- Players can distinguish canon history from replay mastery.
- Players can name one way their run differs from another possible run.

## Stop conditions

Redesign the system when:

- players chase points by creating preventable harm,
- scores reveal a morally preferred story choice,
- the Roadbook feels like homework,
- capability growth becomes repetitive grinding,
- history entries explain more than the player knows,
- the Journey Score overwhelms character consequence,
- the Challenge Board weakens the canon run,
- or players cannot identify why their outcome changed.

## Acceptance statement

The system succeeds when a player can say all of these things.

> I knew what I was trying to do.
>
> I got better at doing it.
>
> I understood what my action changed.
>
> My history belongs to this playthrough.
>
> I have a reason to try another road.
