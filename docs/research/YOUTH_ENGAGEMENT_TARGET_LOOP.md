# Youth Engagement Target Loop

## Goal

The prototype should compete for youth attention with strong commercial narrative games while preserving the project's literary purpose.

The design must feel like a game first.

Literary interpretation should emerge through action, consequence, place, and conversation.

## Primary review question

Would a high-school player choose to continue after the preview ends?

## Target loop

Use this loop for each review round:

1. Build one reviewable artifact.
2. Show it without explaining the intended response.
3. Observe where attention rises or drops.
4. Record player words before interpreting them.
5. Score the experience gates.
6. Identify the weakest gate.
7. Change one main cause.
8. Retest with new participants.

Do not optimize several weak signals at the same time.

A focused change makes the next result easier to interpret.

## Experience gates

### Hook

Target: most players can name a question, danger, relationship, or place they want to understand within five minutes.

Failure signal: players describe the opening as setup that they must endure before the game begins.

### Agency

Target: players identify at least two meaningful actions within the first ten minutes.

Failure signal: players believe the important outcome is fixed regardless of their action.

### Activity clarity

Target: players can explain the main player verbs without reading a design document.

Failure signal: players describe the experience only as walking and choosing dialogue.

### Character pull

Target: players want to spend more time with at least two characters for different reasons.

Failure signal: every relationship reduces to like, dislike, victim, or villain.

### Place pull

Target: players want to revisit one familiar place and explore one unknown place.

Failure signal: locations function only as mission containers.

### Curiosity

Target: players voluntarily ask a question that the preview does not answer.

Failure signal: the preview explains every mystery before interest can form.

### Consequence legibility

Target: players can describe one way an action changed a practical or social situation.

Failure signal: players notice branching dialogue but not changed relationships or access.

### Emotional range

Target: the preview contains tension, ordinary warmth, humor, competence, and uncertainty.

Failure signal: every scene uses the same emotional pressure.

### Literary depth

Target: players who know the memoir recognize its concerns without watching a direct reenactment.

Failure signal: source fidelity depends on copied plot beats or exposition.

### Non-reader access

Target: players who do not know the memoir understand the immediate stakes.

Failure signal: the preview assumes knowledge that only a reader has.

### Youth respect

Target: players report that the game trusts them to notice, infer, and decide.

Failure signal: the game explains its themes like a lesson after each scene.

## Quantitative targets

Use numbers as review signals, not as proof of quality.

- At least 80 percent can state what they were trying to accomplish in the main gameplay sequence.
- At least 75 percent can name one consequence caused by their action.
- At least 70 percent want to continue immediately after the preview.
- At least 70 percent identify one character they want to understand better.
- At least 70 percent identify one location they want to explore.
- At least 60 percent voluntarily mention an unresolved question or mystery.
- Fewer than 20 percent describe the experience as mostly cutscenes or exposition.
- Fewer than 20 percent say the educational purpose is more visible than the game purpose.

## Review groups

Run separate sessions when possible.

- High-school students who know *Educated*.
- High-school students who do not know *Educated*.
- Players who prefer action games.
- Players who prefer narrative games.
- Rural-work reviewers.
- Faith reviewers.
- Accessibility reviewers.
- Trauma-informed reviewers.

Do not average away a serious representation or accessibility problem.

## Stop conditions

Stop and redesign the preview when two consecutive rounds fail the same core gate.

Stop expansion when players like the story but do not want to perform the gameplay verbs.

Stop source expansion when additional memoir detail makes the fictional world feel less coherent.

## Green-light condition

Move to the interaction prototype when the preview passes Hook, Agency, Activity Clarity, Character Pull, and Curiosity together.

The team must also resolve any critical rights, accessibility, or content-care issue before prototype expansion.
