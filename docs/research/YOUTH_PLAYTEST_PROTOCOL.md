# Youth Playtest Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol tests whether *Beyond the Ridge* earns attention through play rather than literary obligation.

Use it for the trailer, gameplay preview, and interaction prototype.

## Participant groups

Recruit separate groups when possible:

- High-school students who know *Educated*.
- High-school students who do not know *Educated*.
- Players who prefer action games.
- Players who prefer narrative games.
- Players who rarely choose narrative games.

Keep faith, rural-work, accessibility, and content-care reviews separate from popularity scoring.

A representation problem must not disappear inside an average score.

## Session structure

### 1. Baseline interview — 3 minutes

Ask:

- What games hold your attention?
- What makes you stop playing a story game?
- What do you expect when a game says it is inspired by a book?

Do not explain the design goals.

### 2. Blind viewing or play — 3 to 20 minutes

Show only the artifact under test.

Do not explain controls, themes, or source connections unless the artifact requires that information.

Record:

- First voluntary question.
- First visible confusion.
- First moment of delight or surprise.
- First attention drop.
- First action the participant wants to try.

### 3. Immediate recall — 5 minutes

Ask:

- What were you trying to do?
- What could you have done differently?
- Who interested you most?
- Where would you go next?
- What changed because of a choice?
- What are you still wondering about?

Record the participant's words before interpreting them.

### 4. Source check — 4 minutes

For readers, ask what felt connected to *Educated*.

For non-readers, ask whether any part seemed to require book knowledge.

Do not tell participants which details came from the memoir.

### 5. Continuation test — 2 minutes

Ask:

`If the game continued right now, would you keep playing?`

Then ask:

`What would you hope to do next?`

The second answer matters more than a yes or no.

## Observer rules

Do not rescue a participant from mild confusion too quickly.

Do not praise an answer.

Do not explain why a mechanic matters.

Do not ask whether the participant noticed a specific feature before open recall ends.

Separate observed behavior from reviewer interpretation.

## Review output

Complete `youth-playtest-scorecard.csv` after each session.

Then record:

- Strongest hook.
- Weakest gate.
- One probable cause.
- One change to test next.
- One feature that must remain unchanged during the next test.

## Iteration rule

Change the weakest experience gate before adding more content.

Do not solve a pacing failure by adding another mechanic.

Do not solve a clarity failure by adding more explanatory text.

Do not solve low curiosity by adding a larger mystery before the existing mystery becomes legible.
