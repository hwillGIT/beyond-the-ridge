# Visual Proof of Play

## Purpose

This phase tests whether *Beyond the Ridge* reads as an engaging game before engine production begins.

The work proceeds in this order:

1. Produce 12 locked gameplay keyframes.
2. Build the game-design card library.
3. Assemble the 90-second concept trailer.
4. Assemble the three-minute gameplay preview.
5. Run structured youth reviews.
6. Revise the weakest experience signal.
7. Repeat the review until the quality gates pass or the team stops the concept.

## Twelve gameplay keyframes

Each frame must show a player activity, a pressure, an alternative action, and a human consequence.

| ID | Frame | Player proof | Human proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| KF-01 | Ridge Dawn | Explore and choose what to notice | Home and the outside world compete for attention |
| KF-02 | Breakfast Pressure | Listen, answer, deflect, or remain silent | Family roles shape the workday |
| KF-03 | Hairline Crack | Inspect, mark, repair, report, or ignore | Competence can conflict with authority |
| KF-04 | Suspended Load | Stabilize, cut power, pull clear, or call out | Action changes trust and blame |
| KF-05 | Mercy House | Prepare care, document facts, or call outside help | Care, belief, and uncertainty coexist |
| KF-06 | County Road | Drive, observe, and talk while moving | Small talk reveals memory and reputation |
| KF-07 | Cedar Gate | Navigate errands, places, and social attention | The town knows the family before Mara speaks |
| KF-08 | Supper Version | Correct, soften, support, or stay silent | The day becomes a contested family story |
| KF-09 | Memory Journal | Separate Saw, Heard, and Concluded | The player can revise an interpretation |
| KF-10 | Ridge Signal | Track, prepare, turn back, or ask for help | Curiosity creates social cost |
| KF-11 | College Threshold | Navigate unfamiliar systems and language | New knowledge changes what Mara can name |
| KF-12 | Wider World | Compare old and new ways of seeing | Expansion creates possibility and loss |

## Keyframe acceptance rules

A frame passes only when a viewer can answer four questions without a caption.

- What is Mara doing?
- What can go wrong?
- Who cares about the result?
- What other action appears possible?

A beautiful image that cannot answer these questions is concept art, not gameplay proof.

## Game-design card library

The card library converts the large bibles into small production references.

Create these card families:

- 13 character cards.
- 15 location cards.
- 10 system cards.
- 8 encounter cards.
- 5 worldview cards.
- 6 relationship-state cards.
- 12 gameplay-keyframe cards.

Each card uses one stable visual grammar and one stable vocabulary.

Each card identifies its canonical source file.

## Ninety-second trailer

The trailer must sell the emotional and sensory promise without explaining the whole design.

The trailer must show:

- Mountain scale.
- Practical work.
- Family warmth and pressure.
- Readable danger.
- Humor or ordinary life.
- A wider social world.
- Contested memory.
- Curiosity that opens a route.

The trailer must not imply that college is the only goal.

## Three-minute gameplay preview

The gameplay preview must show continuous player control.

It should include traversal, repair, interruption, dialogue during work, consequence, and the memory journal.

The preview should use minimal cuts.

A viewer must understand what the player does without a narrator explaining every mechanic.

## Production gate

Do not start an engine prototype because the images look attractive.

Start the prototype only when review participants want to perform the actions shown in the preview.
