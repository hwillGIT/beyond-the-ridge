# Motion Study Specifications

## Purpose

These six motion studies bridge static previsualization and the first interactive prototype.

Each study isolates one player action that must remain readable while social or environmental pressure changes.

A motion study is successful when a reviewer can describe the action, interruption, alternative, and consequence without a design explanation.

## Shared rules

- Keep Mara visually consistent across all studies.
- Use grounded 1999 materials, tools, vehicles, signs, and interfaces.
- Show cause and effect before adding cinematic flourish.
- Keep the camera close enough to read hands, objects, and human reactions.
- Do not use floating quest icons or unknown-future outcome labels.
- Do not convert failure into a reload prompt when the situation can change instead.
- Preserve at least one credible alternative action in every study.

## MS-01 — Axle inspection

**Player verb:** inspect and diagnose.

**Target duration:** 12 to 18 seconds.

**Sequence:**

1. Brush dirt from the axle housing.
2. Mark the suspect seam with chalk.
3. Rotate the hub and compare the mark.
4. Stop work when the mark shifts.
5. Decide whether to reinforce, challenge the order, ask for help, or continue.

**Readability requirement:** the player must understand why the crack matters before dialogue resolves the argument.

**Human pressure:** Abel wants speed and compliance.

**Fail-forward result:** continuing work may preserve approval while increasing later mechanical risk.

## MS-02 — Emergency stop

**Player verb:** interrupt dangerous machinery.

**Target duration:** 8 to 12 seconds.

**Sequence:**

1. Notice chain twist and worker position.
2. See the suspended load shift.
3. Reach the emergency stop.
4. Halt the machine.
5. Read the reactions before choosing what to say.

**Readability requirement:** the load, worker, stop control, and escape space must remain visible in one coherent geography.

**Human pressure:** stopping work protects someone but challenges authority and schedule.

**Fail-forward result:** a slow response can change injury risk, trust, workload, and the household mood without forcing a reload.

## MS-03 — Drive and talk

**Player verb:** drive while listening and choosing what to reveal.

**Target duration:** 20 to 30 seconds.

**Sequence:**

1. Follow the county road toward Cedar Gate.
2. Track weather, traffic, and familiar landmarks.
3. Hear Caleb ask a personal question.
4. Keep control of the vehicle while deciding whether to answer.
5. Arrive with the conversation unresolved or changed.

**Readability requirement:** driving must remain an active task rather than a cinematic background.

**Human pressure:** Caleb offers ordinary outside-world care that Mara may experience as intrusive, relieving, or dangerous.

**Fail-forward result:** silence, partial truth, humor, or disclosure changes later access and conversation without grading the player morally.

## MS-04 — Cedar Gate navigation

**Player verb:** choose a destination through learned geography.

**Target duration:** 20 to 30 seconds.

**Sequence:**

1. Enter the one-stoplight town.
2. Recognize the fuel counter, opera house, library, hardware store, and meetinghouse.
3. Park and notice who recognizes the Vale family.
4. Turn physically toward more than one useful destination.
5. Commit to a route without a floating quest marker.

**Readability requirement:** players should learn the town by landmarks and repeated travel.

**Human pressure:** being known creates access, gossip, obligation, and scrutiny.

**Fail-forward result:** choosing one destination changes what information, tools, and relationships are available before supper.

## MS-05 — Evidence journal

**Player verb:** separate observation, testimony, and interpretation.

**Target duration:** 20 to 30 seconds.

**Sequence:**

1. Open the field notebook after the day ends.
2. Place physical evidence and remembered statements into distinct spaces.
3. Notice a contradiction.
4. Revise one conclusion without erasing the earlier conclusion.
5. Leave one unresolved item beside the map or next-day plan.

**Readability requirement:** revision must feel like thinking with evidence rather than filling out a menu.

**Human pressure:** changing a conclusion may conflict with loyalty, fear, or a previous promise.

**Fail-forward result:** uncertainty can unlock a question, route, or conversation instead of withholding progress.

## MS-06 — College threshold

**Player verb:** learn an unfamiliar institutional rule.

**Target duration:** 25 to 40 seconds.

**Sequence:**

1. Enter campus with a paper schedule and incomplete assumptions.
2. Take the wrong bus or route.
3. Recognize the mistake from environmental evidence.
4. Ask another student or consult a directory.
5. Reach the correct room with new practical knowledge.

**Readability requirement:** confusion must be recoverable through observation, asking, and persistence.

**Human pressure:** Mara's knowledge gap creates embarrassment without treating her as unintelligent.

**Fail-forward result:** the mistake costs time or confidence but teaches a reusable rule.

## Review gate

Do not move these studies into an engine because the images look convincing.

Move forward when youth reviewers can explain what they would do, what could go wrong, and why another player might choose differently.

The strongest studies should create this response without exposing a morality score or an explicit lesson statement.
