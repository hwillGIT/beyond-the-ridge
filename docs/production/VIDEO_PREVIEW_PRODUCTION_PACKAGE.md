# Video Preview Production Package

## Status

This package is ready for picture editing, sound assembly, caption work, and review rendering.

The repository does not contain a finished video.

The package supports two outputs:

- a 90-second concept trailer,
- and a three-minute gameplay preview.

## Production goal

The videos must prove that *Beyond the Ridge* is a game.

They must show physical action, meaningful choice, risk, consequence, score, history, and reasons to continue.

The videos must not rely on literary familiarity.

A viewer who has not read *Educated* must understand the immediate task and pressure.

They can support establishing shots and emotional transitions.

They do not prove player control by themselves.

- and evidence review.

### Contextual-interface frames

These frames establish:

## Picture-edit method

### Step 1: action spine

Build the first cut around player action.

Use these verbs:

- inspect,
- move,
- choose,
- brace,
- perform,
- warn,
- drive,
- report,
- revise,
- and replay.

Do not begin with long exposition.

### Step 2: human pressure

Add character reactions around the action.

Use posture, eye line, distance, interruption, silence, and forms of address.

Do not explain every relationship through dialogue.

### Step 3: consequence

Show what changed.

Possible changes include:

- equipment condition,
- injury,
- access,
- time,
- trust,
- evidence,
- public account,
- and route availability.

Do not reveal unknown future results.

### Step 4: record

Add score or history after the world communicates the result.

The score confirms craft.

The history records competing accounts.

Neither system replaces the scene.

### Step 5: forward pull

End each major section with a new question, risk, route, capability, or relationship move.

The final beat should create a desire to act again.

## Trailer editorial grammar

The trailer can compress distance, time, and campaign scope.

It must still preserve cause and effect.

Use one visual question at a time.

Use no more than five complete spoken lines.

Use no more than three title cards.

Show the player’s hands before showing a score.

Show a disputed account before showing Living History.

Show loss before hinting at a Second Road.

## Gameplay-preview editorial grammar

The gameplay preview must feel continuous.

Use practical transitions between locations.

Show a meaningful player action at least every 20 seconds.

Do not count camera movement alone.

Do not hide all interaction changes behind cuts.

Show the Canon Mark before the Challenge Board.

Keep the Best Mark empty until replay.

## Interface integration

### Low density

Use during exploration, arrival, and mystery.

Show one objective, one nearby action, and one relevant condition.

### Contextual density

Use during driving, conversation, town activity, and home scenes.

Show intentions, route state, time pressure, or one active tool.

### Full density

Use during repair, danger, care, score, and evidence analysis.

Show only the panels needed for the current action.

### Player Record inserts

Use the Encounter Scorecard after the Hairline Crack.

Use Living History after the supper retelling.

Use the Challenge Board at the gameplay-preview ending.

Use the Legacy Card only as a trailer fragment.

## Sound package

Build the sound bed from the world.

Primary sound groups are:

- mountain wind,
- distant road noise,
- railcar movement,
- plates and room tone,
- tools and metal strain,
- engines and tires,
- rain and traction,
- glass and herbal preparation,
- telephone and radio lines,
- paper and pencil,
- footsteps and public interiors,
- and restrained music.

Use sound to warn the player before a visual alert.

Keep the chain change audible before the load shifts.

Keep tire noise readable before the vehicle loses control.

Keep radio static directional during Ridge Signal.

## Dialogue package

Use original game dialogue.

Do not quote the memoir.

Keep trailer dialogue sparse.

Keep gameplay dialogue inside ongoing activity.

Preserve each character’s established speech register.

Confirm names and forms of address against the source records.

## Caption package

Create an SRT file for each video.

Caption:

- spoken dialogue,
- radio speech,
- critical off-screen warnings,
- and meaningful sound cues.

Use speaker names when the speaker is not visually clear.

Keep captions away from action prompts and score text.

Review reading duration at normal speed.

## Accessibility review

Check:

- text size,
- contrast,
- caption placement,
- sound-only warnings,
- color-only warnings,
- camera shake,
- rapid flashes,
- rain visibility,
- interface dwell time,
- and intense scene transitions.

Create a reduced-motion review export when requested.

Accessibility options do not lower score or difficulty.

## Rights and source review

Do not include:

- the uploaded memoir,
- page scans,
- publisher covers,
- audiobook material,
- copied dialogue,
- or protected photographs.

Use fictional names and transformed places.

Replace direct institutional branding before publication.

Record any unresolved rights concern in review notes.

## Review sequence

### Editorial review

Confirm timing, continuity, cause, and forward pull.

### Gameplay review

Confirm visible control, alternatives, pressure, and consequence.

### Interface review

Confirm text, hierarchy, duration, and canonical terminology.

### Sound review

Confirm dialogue, danger cues, world tone, and silence.

### Source-fidelity review

Confirm transformed influence and correct character detail.

### Content-care review

Confirm trauma, family, faith, rural work, injury, and loss treatment.

### Accessibility review

Confirm captions, contrast, motion, sound cues, and text duration.

### Youth review

Show the video without an explanation.

Ask viewers what they want to do next.

Record their words before interpreting the result.

## Acceptance scorecard

The concept trailer passes when viewers can name:

- one action,
- one danger,
- one relationship,
- and one unanswered question.

The gameplay preview passes when viewers can name:

- the main objective,
- four player verbs,
- one practical consequence,
- one social consequence,
- one disputed account,
- and one replay goal.

The package fails when viewers describe only photography or plot.

## Render checks

Before approval, confirm:

- correct duration,
- correct dimensions,
- stable frame rate,
- complete audio,
- no clipped captions,
- no temporary brands,
- no legacy names,
- no unreadable generated text,
- no missing source credit,
- and no false engine-capture claim.

- both MP4 files exist,
- both SRT files exist,
- review notes identify the approved versions,
- all review gates pass,
