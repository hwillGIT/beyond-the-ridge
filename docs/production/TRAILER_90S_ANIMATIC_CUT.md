# 90-Second Trailer Animatic Cut

## Purpose

This cut proves the emotional arc before final voice, music, motion, or engine footage.

It uses the 12 approved gameplay keyframes.

The cut is previsualization. It must not imply that the project has rendered gameplay.

## Emotional progression

Use this progression:

`Awe -> Belonging -> Competence -> Danger -> Care -> Openness -> Exposure -> Doubt -> Interpretation -> Curiosity -> Disorientation -> Possibility`

The trailer should not explain the plot.

It should make the viewer want to know what Mara will do next.

## Sound direction

The first audio pass should use environmental sound before music.

Priority sounds:

- Wind through pine and sheet metal.
- Kitchen dishes and a distant telephone.
- Ratchet clicks and metal strain.
- Chain tension and engine idle.
- Glass jars, water, cloth, and breath.
- Road noise and radio static.
- Store bell, traffic, and distant church ambience.
- Tableware and uncomfortable silence.
- Pencil on paper.
- Ridge wind and radio pulse.
- Bus brakes, footsteps, and fluorescent room tone.
- Stone courtyard ambience and distant bells.

Music should enter late and remain restrained.

## Trailer review gates

A youth viewer should be able to answer these questions after one viewing:

- Who is Mara trying to become?
- What does the player actually do?
- Why can work become dangerous?
- Why does family still matter to Mara?
- What question remains unanswered?
- What new place would the viewer want to enter?

The trailer fails when viewers describe it only as a serious book adaptation.

The trailer succeeds when viewers describe a game they want to enter.
