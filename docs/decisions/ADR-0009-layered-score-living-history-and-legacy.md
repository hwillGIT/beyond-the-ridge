# ADR-0009: Layered score, Living History, and Legacy Card

## Status

Accepted

## Context

The game needs clear feedback, mastery, completion, and replay value.

A single universal score would be easy to understand.

It would also reduce faith, family, trauma, education, loyalty, and survival to one approval number.

Removing all scores would protect nuance but weaken gamer feedback.

The project also needs a stable history that records competing accounts rather than replacing them with an omniscient timeline.

## Decision

Use a layered Player Record.

The system includes:

- twelve-mark encounter scores for observable craft,
- capability mastery through demonstrated proofs,
- tool and route familiarity,
- an optional 1,000-point Journey Score,
- a Living History with competing accounts and revisions,
- a Challenge Board that separates replay mastery from canon history,
- an Ending Archive,
- and an immutable Legacy Card for each completed run.

Do not use a global morality score.

Do not score belief, obedience, trauma, forgiveness, family loyalty, medical vulnerability, leaving home, staying local, attending college, or activating a Redemption Window.

Accessibility settings do not reduce scores.

## Consequences

### Benefits

- Players receive immediate and long-term mastery feedback.
- The game supports personal bests without rewriting the story.
- Capability growth unlocks verbs rather than generic power.
- Players can compare distinct runs.
- The history system preserves uncertainty and disagreement.
- The Legacy Card gives each ending a concrete record.
- The design supports completionists, challenge players, explorers, and narrative players.

### Costs

- The game must store more event and branch state.
- The Roadbook requires careful information design.
- Score dimensions require playtesting for fairness.
- The project must prevent point farming through harmful outcomes.
- The team must distinguish canon marks from replay marks.
- The Journey Score must not dominate character consequence.

## Validation

The decision remains accepted when youth testers can:

- explain why an encounter received its marks,
- identify one capability they want to improve,
- describe one action-to-consequence link,
- distinguish canon history from replay mastery,
- and understand that the Journey Score is not a morality score.

Redesign the system when players create preventable harm to improve a score or when the Roadbook feels like required homework.
